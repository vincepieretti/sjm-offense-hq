Our Offensive IdentityThe DNA we're building from — three influences, one system

Influence 017 Seconds or Less

D'Antoni's Suns: attack before the defense is set. Rebound → outlet → sprint. If the break isn't there, flow into the next action — never stop the ball.

Influence 02Dribble-Drive Motion

Walberg's attack framework: spread the floor, drive gaps relentlessly, kick to shooters, and read the next gap. The drive is the trigger for everything.

Influence 035-Out Motion

All five players start beyond the arc. Constant spacing, cutting, and interchange — every player is a decision-maker, every possession has driving lanes.

Core Rules (Draft — for Wednesday's discussion)

With the Ball

  • Eyes up on the catch. First look: pitch ahead. Second look: attack.
  • 0.5-second decisions — shoot, drive, or move it. Holding the ball is the only mistake.
  • Paint touches create everything. Drive to score first; the kick is the reward for collapsing help.

Without the Ball

  • Sprint to spacing. Wings run wide to the corners; big rim-runs or sets a drag.
  • Fill behind the drive (DDM): baseline drift, 45 lift, corner fill.
  • Screens are options, not scripts — curl, reject, flare, or slip based on the defender.

Breakdown: 7 Seconds or Less Suns OffenseThe Scoring Factory · Ep. 5 · transcript-verified breakdown

▶ 0:00

The Philosophy — "A Mindset, Not Marketing"

▶
Video talking points
  • Attack before the defense is set. The entire system exists to create advantages before coverage can load up.
  • No called set every possession. D'Antoni trusted great players to make quick decisions with space.
  • Faster ball movement = worse defense. "Simple math."
  • If the break isn't there, flow. Don't stop the ball — chain into the next action.
  • Personnel blueprint: Nash orchestrating, Amar'e sprinting rim-to-rim, Joe Johnson & Q-Richardson flying to the corners.
SJM Translation
  • Our version: "7 seconds or flow" — great shot early, or seamless flow into 5-out motion / DDM.
  • Sell the mindset to players first, X's and O's second. This is a freedom pitch — freedom earned by sprinting and spacing.
  • Practice design must reward speed of decision, not just makes.
▶ 1:37

The Primary Break — Outlet, Lanes & Pitch-Aheads

▶
Video talking points
  • Defensive rebound → immediate outlet → go. No walk-it-up possessions.
  • Lane structure: both wings sprint wide (one to each corner), the big either rim-runs or sets a drag screen for the PG.
  • Eyes up instantly. Nash scanned the floor the moment he received the outlet.
  • Pitch ahead. Throwing the ball ahead to a sprinting teammate is one of the most efficient plays in basketball — beat the defense for dunks and layups, or seal deep under the rim.
  • The counter: teams stopped crashing the offensive glass to get back — which is exactly what forces the secondary break.
SJM Translation
  • Teach the rebound-outlet as one motion; guards to designated outlet zones, no waiting.
  • Wings' rule: "rim or corner in 3 seconds." Nothing in between.
  • Big's read: rim-run if the lane is open, drag if the ball is contained.
  • Drill idea: 5-on-0 → 5-on-3 → 5-on-5 advance-pass ladder; score only counts inside 7 seconds.
▶ 3:17

Secondary Break — Drag Screens & "77"

▶
Video talking points
  • No bucket on the break? Flow instantly into the secondary — signature action: the drag screen (a ball screen set on the move in transition).
  • Why it works: the defense never gets to set its normal ball-screen coverage — the screen arrives before the scheme does.
  • Double drag ("77"): two staggered drags in transition — multiplies confusion and forces switches the defense doesn't want.
  • Guard-to-guard actions early: Marion screening for Nash, or a handoff flowing straight into a pick-and-roll.
SJM Translation
  • Drag = our bridge between the break and 5-out motion. Big's default when the rim-run isn't there.
  • Install "77" as a call and as a read the players can flow into on their own.
  • Guard-to-guard PnR punishes teams that switch small on our drags.
▶ 4:35

Halfcourt Sets — Through Series, Zoom & Flares

▶
Video talking points
  • "Through" series: PG passes off, cuts through the middle, loops back out and gets a screen on the empty side — the empty side is what creates all the options.
  • Reads off that screen: curl it, or come up into a handoff — the pindown-into-handoff is today's "Zoom" action.
  • Rescreen wrinkle: Nash sets a screen himself, goes to get the ball, then gets a rescreen — and the screen can convert into a flare.
  • Zoom + weakside flare: off-ball flares and pindowns on the weak side pull tag defenders away from the main action and add a catch-and-shoot three option.
SJM Translation
  • Through series fits 5-out perfectly — the PG cut empties the slot and keeps the paint clear.
  • Teach reads in this order: curl → zoom → flare → rescreen. One action, four answers.
  • Weakside flares are our counter when teams load up on the strong-side action.
▶ 6:35

The Legacy — "The Future, Played 20 Years Early"

▶
Video talking points
  • Today's Pacers, Celtics, and D'Antoni's Rockets all pull directly from this blueprint.
  • The five pillars that survived: early offense, drag screens, five-out spacing, run-and-gun transition, freedom to play.
  • The Suns never won the title — but they permanently shifted how the league plays.
SJM Translation
  • Those five pillars are basically our program's offensive mission statement — lead with this slide on Wednesday.
  • "Freedom to play" is the recruiting pitch to our players: sprint + space = green light.
